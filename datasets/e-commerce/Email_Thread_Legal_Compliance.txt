Dear Karan,

Further to our call, here are the compliance requirements for the Horizon storefront. Please
have these built into the requirements — most are not optional to operate a consumer store in
India. I've kept it practical rather than a legal essay.

1. POLICY PAGES (all must be live at launch, linked in the footer on every page, and shown/
   linked at checkout):
   - Terms & Conditions / Terms of Use
   - Privacy Policy
   - Return, Refund & Cancellation Policy (must match what Ops/CX actually do — align with
     their intake form; note the return window is still undecided there)
   - Shipping / Delivery Policy (serviceability, timelines, charges)
   - Cookie Policy
   These should be manageable as content (not hard-coded), so we can update wording without a
   developer. I understand there's a CMS requirement already — tie them to that.

2. CONSENT AT CHECKOUT: before an order is placed, the customer must affirmatively accept the
   Terms & the Return Policy (a ticked checkbox with links — not pre-ticked). Record that
   acceptance against the order (timestamp + which version of the terms).

3. COOKIES: a cookie-consent banner on first visit, allowing accept / reject of non-essential
   cookies (analytics, marketing) with essential cookies exempt. Store the consent choice and
   don't fire non-essential trackers before consent. This matters because Growth wants
   analytics — analytics must respect this.

4. DATA PROTECTION (DPDP Act, 2023): we process personal data (name, contact, address, order
   history), so:
   - Collect data only for stated purposes; show the purpose (the Privacy Policy covers this).
   - Obtain consent for processing where required; keep it demonstrable.
   - MARKETING CONSENT must be SEPARATE and OPT-IN. Do not bundle it with account creation or
     T&C acceptance. Promotional emails/SMS/WhatsApp (including things like abandoned-cart or
     promo campaigns) may only go to customers who opted in. Transactional messages (order
     confirmation, shipping, refunds) are fine without marketing consent.
   - Provide an easy unsubscribe / withdraw-consent path.

5. DATA-PRINCIPAL RIGHTS: customers can request access to, correction of, and erasure of their
   personal data. For launch a manual/admin-assisted process is acceptable, but there must be a
   documented way to do it and to respond in reasonable time.

6. GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL: publish contact details of a Grievance Officer (name/role + email) on
   the site, as required. CX (Neha) should own that inbox.

7. RETENTION: retain tax invoices and transaction records for the statutory period required
   under tax law (Priyanka to confirm the exact duration — do not guess). Personal data beyond
   its purpose/retention period should be deleted or anonymised, EXCEPT where we must keep it
   for the tax records above.

Happy to review the policy copy before go-live. Please confirm these are captured.
